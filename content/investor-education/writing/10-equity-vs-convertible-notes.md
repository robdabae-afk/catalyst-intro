# Equity versus convertible notes

Equity, convertible notes, and SAFEs are different legal instruments. Equity represents shares issued to an investor. The class matters: common and preferred shares can have different economic, voting, or liquidation rights. A convertible note is generally a debt obligation, often with interest and a maturity date, and may convert into equity when a specified financing or other event occurs. A SAFE is generally an agreement for future equity, not a conventional loan with interest and maturity.

For a fictional illustration, a $1,000 convertible note at 6% simple annual interest would accrue $60 over one year if the agreement uses that rate and calculation. If it converts, the contract may say the principal plus accrued interest converts at a discount or cap-based price. If it reaches maturity without conversion, the company may owe repayment under the note, but a cash-strapped startup might not be able to pay. The note’s legal priority does not guarantee recovery if the company fails.

Read the actual instrument for its interest, maturity, conversion triggers, valuation cap or discount, repayment terms, and treatment in a sale or dissolution. Compare the note with existing securities and the company’s ability to meet obligations. Do not assume “convertible” means conversion is certain, or that a SAFE is equivalent to a note. Different templates can produce meaningfully different outcomes. If repayment priority or conversion math is unclear, get qualified legal advice before making a decision.
