# Startup investments can be illiquid

Liquidity is about access to cash, not whether a company has a high valuation on paper. Publicly traded shares can usually be sold through a market during trading hours. A private startup investment generally has no comparable exchange. If you need your money for an unexpected bill, the company may not be able to return it.

For a fictional example, Jamal invests $1,000 in a Reg CF security and decides six months later that he needs the cash. Reg CF securities generally cannot be resold during the first year, except for specified transfers such as transfers to the issuer, certain accredited investors, family members, or in a registered offering. Even when a transfer is legally permitted, the company’s governing documents may impose additional limits, and Jamal still needs a willing buyer. After one year, there is no promise of a market.

An acquisition or initial public offering can sometimes create a path to liquidity, but neither is assured. A company may instead keep operating privately, raise more money, or close. An increase in a financing valuation does not mean an investor can sell at that price. Review transfer terms and ask whether the security has any company-approval requirement. Treat the possible holding period as indefinite, and do not invest money you may need soon.
