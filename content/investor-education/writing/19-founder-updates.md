# Reading founder updates

Founder updates can give context between formal disclosures, but they are not automatically audited, complete, or a substitute for required filings. Read each update for the date range, definitions, and whether metrics are actual results or targets. Use the same definitions over time where possible. A company that reports “users,” for example, should clarify whether those are paying customers, free sign-ups, active users, or something else.

Fictional example: a company reports 250 customers at the end of one quarter and 325 at the end of the next. The increase is 75 customers. Dividing 75 by 250 gives 30% growth. That tells you customer count rose by 30% under the company’s definition. It does not tell you revenue per customer, whether those customers are still active, the cost to acquire them, or whether the business is profitable.

Compare progress with the milestones and use of proceeds in the offering materials. If a product launch is delayed, look for what changed, what the company learned, and how the revised plan affects cash needs. Reg CF issuers generally have annual reporting requirements under Rule 202, subject to exceptions and conditions. Check official filings for required reports and amendments instead of relying only on informal updates. Save updates and compare them over time. Clear reporting can improve your understanding, but it does not eliminate business risk or make a future return predictable.

*Educational information only. This is not legal, tax, or investment advice. Examples are fictional and simplified.*
