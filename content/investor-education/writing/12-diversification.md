# Diversification and startup risk

Diversification spreads exposure across investments so that one company or asset does not determine the whole result. The SEC’s investor education materials explain that diversification may reduce risk but cannot guarantee against loss. That distinction matters especially with startups: businesses can fail, and companies in the same sector may be affected by the same market conditions.

Here is a fictional arithmetic example. Investor A puts $1,000 into one startup. If it becomes worthless, the investment value is $0. Investor B divides $1,000 equally among ten different startups, investing $100 in each. If one investment becomes worthless and the other nine are each still worth exactly $100, the portfolio is worth $900 before fees and taxes. The $100 loss is smaller than Investor A’s $1,000 loss in this scenario.

This is not a forecast. The nine remaining companies could also decline or fail, and the companies may be correlated. A portfolio of ten early-stage companies can still be a concentrated, high-risk portfolio compared with a person’s total financial resources. Diversification across startup names is not the same as having emergency savings, a suitable time horizon, or a balanced allocation among asset types. Consider how much you can afford to put at risk overall, and avoid assuming that a larger number of investments guarantees safety.
