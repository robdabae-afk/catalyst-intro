# A practical due-diligence routine

Due diligence is not a guarantee that an investment will succeed. It is a process for checking what can be checked before taking risk. The SEC recommends researching investment opportunities and reviewing company information. Reg CF offerings provide formal disclosures, and a registered intermediary has a specific role in the transaction.

A useful routine is to translate the company’s pitch into plain questions. What product or service is sold? Who is the customer? What proof shows the customer wants it? How does the company earn revenue? What must happen for the next milestone? Then compare the marketing material with the Form C and exhibits. Check financial statement dates, whether the statements are certified, reviewed, or audited, and whether the numbers match the narrative. Review use of proceeds and related-party transactions. Search EDGAR for filings and amendments. For a Reg CF intermediary, verify its status through SEC and FINRA resources, including BrokerCheck for brokers.

For example, if a pitch says “more than 1,000 customers,” clarify whether that means paying customers, trial users, or sign-ups, and what period the number covers. An unexplained difference is a question to resolve, not proof of misconduct. Keep notes on each assumption and unanswered question. If the company does not provide a material fact or you cannot explain the security’s terms, you can choose not to proceed. Taking time to research is not the same as missing an opportunity; it is part of making an informed decision.
