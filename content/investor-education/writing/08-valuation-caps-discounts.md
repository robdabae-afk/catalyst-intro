# Valuation caps and discounts

This fictional illustration isolates arithmetic, not an investor’s guaranteed return or ownership. Assume an $8 million valuation cap, a $20 million priced financing, ten million fully diluted pre-conversion shares, and a $500 SAFE. For this simplified denominator, $8,000,000 divided by 10,000,000 shares is $0.80 per share. The priced round’s illustrative share price is $20,000,000 divided by 10,000,000, or $2.00. Dividing $500 by $0.80 gives 625 shares; dividing $500 by $2.00 gives 250 shares.

If the same SAFE had a 20% discount to the $2.00 round price, the discount price would be $2.00 times 80%, or $1.60 per share. A SAFE with both a cap and discount often has specific language for applying the more favorable conversion price, but the precise formula and capitalization definition in the agreement control.

The example intentionally ignores the new money in the priced round, other SAFEs and notes, option-pool increases, accrued items, and any distinction between pre- and post-money SAFE calculations. Those factors can change the actual conversion price and ownership. The share count alone also does not tell you voting, liquidation, or other rights. Do not divide a cap by your investment to claim a guaranteed company percentage. Ask for the exact contract, definitions, and capitalization assumptions, and have a qualified professional explain any term you do not understand.
