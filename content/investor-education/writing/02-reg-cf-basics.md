# Regulation Crowdfunding basics

Reg CF is a securities offering framework, not a general crowdfunding donation drive. The issuer and intermediary must follow federal requirements, including disclosures and investor protections. The SEC describes the basic framework and links to issuer and investor materials on its Regulation Crowdfunding page.

For example, a fictional company called Harbor Oven wants to raise capital by selling securities. It cannot conduct a Reg CF offering solely through its own website. The offering must use a registered broker-dealer or funding portal, and the company must provide required disclosures. A customer who buys a product from Harbor Oven is not necessarily an investor; an investor receives the specific security named in the offering documents.

The issuer’s maximum under Reg CF is generally $5 million in a rolling twelve-month period, subject to the detailed rules. That is a ceiling, not a target or a signal that the company has passed an SEC quality review. An investor may be subject to a separate individual limit based on income and net worth. Those individual limits aggregate investments across Reg CF offerings, not just one company or one platform.

Reg CF securities generally have a one-year resale restriction, with limited exceptions. This is separate from the practical problem of finding a buyer later. Read the offering terms and verify that the intermediary is registered before entering payment information.
