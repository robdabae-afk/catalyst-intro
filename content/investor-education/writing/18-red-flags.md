# Red flags and claims worth checking

A red flag is a signal to slow down and verify, not a conclusion that someone has committed fraud. Investor.gov highlights warnings such as guaranteed high returns with little or no risk, pressure to act now, fear of missing out, fake testimonials, and suspicious payment methods. In startup offerings, also watch for conflicts between marketing claims and formal disclosures, missing dates on financial data, unclear use of proceeds, or a security that no one can explain plainly.

Suppose a fictional founder says “revenue doubled” in a video, while the offering document shows flat sales. The mismatch could have an innocent explanation, such as different reporting periods or a different revenue measure, but you should ask for the exact basis. If the answer remains unclear, do not fill in the gap with a hopeful assumption. Likewise, confirm that the offering and payment flow are on the official platform of a registered Reg CF intermediary. Do not send money to a personal account or rely on a link from an unknown message.

An SEC filing is not a government recommendation or assurance that an investment is safe. Review the company’s filing and verify intermediaries through official sources. Be especially wary when someone discourages you from reading documents or insists that a decision must happen immediately. A real opportunity can withstand reasonable questions. If you feel pressured, pausing or walking away is an available choice.
