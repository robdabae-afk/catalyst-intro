# Pre-money and post-money valuation

Pre-money and post-money describe when a valuation is measured relative to a financing. Pre-money is the negotiated company value immediately before the new investment. Post-money is measured after adding the new financing, in a simplified calculation. This vocabulary matters because the same headline valuation can imply different ownership for the new money.

Fictional example: a company raises $2 million at an $8 million pre-money valuation. The simple post-money figure is $8 million plus $2 million, or $10 million. The new investors’ portion is $2 million divided by $10 million, or 20%, before accounting for option-pool changes, other securities, or deal-specific terms. If the phrase “$8 million valuation” meant post-money instead, then the implied pre-money figure would be $6 million, and $2 million would be 25% of $8 million.

These are simplified financing calculations, not a promise of ownership or a measure of what shares can later be sold for. The company’s capitalization definition can include outstanding shares, options, SAFEs, notes, and other items in different ways. A SAFE cap is not automatically the same as a priced-round pre-money valuation. Ask the company to specify whether the figure is pre- or post-money, what share count it uses, and whether the option pool is measured before or after the financing. A negotiated valuation is not a guarantee of value or return.
